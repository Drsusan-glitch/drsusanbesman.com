A new year can feel like pressure: new goals, new habits, a new you by February. After a divorce, that pressure can feel especially heavy. You may still be grieving, adjusting, and simply getting through the day.

This year, instead of resolutions, I invite you to try intentions. A resolution is a rule you can break. An intention is a direction you can return to, again and again, with kindness.

1. Look back before you look forward

Before setting anything new, take a few minutes to honor what you’ve already done. Write down three things you got through this year, three things you learned about yourself, and one thing you’re ready to leave behind. You may be surprised by how much strength is on that page.

2. Choose a word for the year

Some women find it helpful to pick one word to guide them: calm, courage, home, joy, trust, or simply “me.” When you face a decision, ask whether it moves you toward that word.

3. Set small, specific intentions

Big goals can feel overwhelming when your energy is low. Try intentions that are small enough to start this week:

  • “I intend to walk outside three mornings a week.”
  • “I intend to call one friend every Sunday.”
  • “I intend to say no to one thing each week that drains me.”
  • “I intend to learn one new thing about my finances each month.”

4. Plan for the hard days

Intentions aren’t about perfection. Some weeks you’ll follow through and some weeks you won’t. Decide now what you’ll do when you slip: notice it without judgment, and simply begin again the next day.

You don’t need a new you. You need room to become more fully yourself.

5. Design one thing to look forward to

Put something on the calendar that is just for you: a trip, a class, a day at the beach, a dinner with friends. Having something to anticipate is good for the heart.

6. Ask for support

Share your intentions with someone you trust, or bring them to therapy. Support makes change more likely to last.
