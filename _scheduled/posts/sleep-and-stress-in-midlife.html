You’re exhausted all day, and then you lie awake at night with your mind racing. If stress and poor sleep have become a cycle, you’re in good company. Sleep problems are especially common in midlife, and during big life changes like divorce, they often get worse.

Why sleep gets harder in midlife

Several things can pile up at once: hormonal changes around perimenopause and menopause, stress and worry, changes in routine, and sometimes more alcohol or caffeine than usual to cope. Poor sleep then makes stress harder to handle, which makes sleep harder still.

The encouraging news is that small, steady changes can break the cycle.

1. Anchor your wake-up time

Getting up at the same time every day, even after a bad night, is one of the most effective ways to reset your body clock. Get daylight in your eyes within an hour of waking, even for ten minutes.

2. Create a wind-down hour

Your brain needs a runway to land. In the hour before bed, dim the lights, put screens away if you can, and choose something calming: a warm shower, gentle stretching, reading, or soft music.

3. Give your worries somewhere to go

If your mind starts listing tomorrow’s problems the moment your head hits the pillow, try this: earlier in the evening, write down what’s on your mind and one next step for each. Your brain is more willing to let go when it knows the worries are captured.

4. Use your breath

When you’re lying awake, try breathing in for four counts and out for six. A longer exhale tells your body it’s safe to settle. If you’re still awake after about twenty minutes, get up, do something quiet in dim light, and return to bed when you feel sleepy.

Rest isn’t a reward for getting everything done. It’s what helps you get through.

5. Watch the hidden sleep-stealers

  • Caffeine after noon
  • Alcohol in the evening, which can disrupt sleep later in the night
  • Late, heavy meals
  • Long naps late in the day

6. Move your body during the day

Regular movement, especially outdoors and earlier in the day, helps both sleep and mood. Strength training is particularly valuable for women in midlife.

When to talk to a professional

If sleep problems last more than a few weeks, talk with your doctor, especially if you snore loudly, wake gasping, or have hot flashes that wake you, since these can have medical causes. If stress, anxiety or grief is keeping you awake, therapy can help too.
