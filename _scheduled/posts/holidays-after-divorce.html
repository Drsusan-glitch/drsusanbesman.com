The first holidays after a divorce can feel like walking through a house full of memories. Traditions that once felt warm can suddenly feel heavy, and the calendar seems to fill with reminders of what has changed.

If you’re dreading this season, you’re not doing anything wrong. Holidays tend to magnify whatever we’re carrying. The good news is that a little planning, and a lot of permission, can make them gentler.

1. Lower the bar on purpose

This doesn’t have to be the best holiday ever. It only has to be one you get through with some kindness toward yourself. Decide ahead of time what really matters to you this year, and let the rest go. Store-bought pie counts. Skipping the big party counts. Saying no counts.

2. Make a plan for the hardest days

Look at the calendar and circle the days you expect to be hardest: the holiday itself, a family gathering, the day the children are with your former spouse. Then put something kind on each one, like a walk with a friend, a movie you love, or volunteering for a few hours. A plan turns an empty day into one with shape.

3. Keep some traditions, change some, and start one

You don’t have to choose between holding on and letting go. Keep one tradition that still feels good. Change one that now hurts. And start one that is entirely yours, like a solo morning hike, a new recipe, or a gathering with friends who feel like family.

4. If you’re sharing the children, keep it about them

Agree on the schedule early and in writing, and try to stick to it. Help your children look forward to time with their other parent, even if part of you aches. If you’ll be apart from them on the day itself, plan your own celebration together before or after. Children remember how a holiday felt far more than what date it fell on.

The holiday doesn’t have to look the way it used to in order to be meaningful.

5. Expect waves of grief, and let them pass through

A song in a store or an ornament in a box can bring tears out of nowhere. That isn’t a setback. It’s grief doing what grief does. When a wave comes, try slowing your breath, in for four counts and out for six, and remind yourself that feelings rise and fall.

6. Go easy on alcohol and isolation

Both can feel comforting in the moment and make the next day harder. Say yes to at least a few invitations, even short ones, and set a limit you feel good about before you arrive.
